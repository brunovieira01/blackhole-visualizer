A one-time tour of the controls. You can reopen this any time from the tray.

Where everything lives

There is no settings window. The full menu — modes, themes, volume, lyrics, the orbit launcher, quality and autostart — lives in the tray icon, at the bottom-right of the taskbar next to the clock. On Windows 11 it usually starts hidden: click the ^ arrow to the left of the tray icons to find it, or pin it for good under Settings → Personalization → Taskbar → Other system tray icons.

The three modes

Switch between them from the mode row in the HUD, top-left of the window — or from the tray menu, or with Ctrl Alt W.

Window The default. An ordinary window, with the HUD.
Wallpaper Becomes your literal desktop background; icons stay on top and keep working, and it never appears in Alt+Tab. Nothing on screen can be clicked down there, so Ctrl Alt W and the tray are how you come back.
Overlay Fullscreen and click-through — only the glow is drawn, the rest of your screen shows through.

What this touches on your PC

Short list, and it is the whole list. No registry keys, no system settings, no files outside the two places below.

Its settings One config.json in this app's own folder under %APPDATA%. Nothing else is written anywhere.
Start with Windows Off unless you turn it on. It is a shortcut in your Startup folder — no service, no scheduled task.
Desktop icons Only the orbit launcher hides them, only if you say yes when asked, and they come back when the app quits. It is the same switch as right-click desktop → View → Show desktop icons.

All of it is undone in one go from the tray: Undo changes to this PC. Uninstalling removes the settings too.

It listens to your speakers, not your microphone. Audio comes from WASAPI loopback, which captures what is already being played and never opens an input device. Nothing is recorded and nothing leaves your machine — the one thing that goes out is a lyrics lookup, by song title, when lyrics are switched on.
